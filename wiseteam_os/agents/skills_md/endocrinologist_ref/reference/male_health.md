# Male Hormonal Health

## Testosterone

### Reference ranges and units

**Total testosterone:**
- Lab reference range (adult man): ~264–916 ng/dL (~9–32 nmol/L). Exact limits depend on
  the lab and method; LC-MS/MS is more accurate than immunoassay.
- "Optimum" by associations with well-being: the middle to upper half of the range, a
  rough target of ≥ 450–500 ng/dL (≥ 15–17 nmol/L). Not dogma – treat symptoms, not a
  number.
- Conversion: ng/dL × 0.0347 = nmol/L.

**Free testosterone** – the biologically active fraction:
- Most accurate is the calculated value via the Vermeulen formula (requires total T,
  SHBG, and albumin) or equilibrium dialysis. Direct immunoassay of free T is unreliable
  and is best not used for decisions.
- As a rough guide, calculated free T below ~6.5 ng/dL (~225 pmol/L) is considered low;
  thresholds depend on method and lab.

**SHBG:** ~10–57 nmol/L. Rises with hyperthyroidism, liver disease, estrogens, aging,
and caloric deficit; falls with obesity, insulin resistance, hypothyroidism, and androgen
excess. High SHBG "binds" testosterone → normal total T with low free T. Therefore, when
symptoms of hypogonadism are present, always look at SHBG and free T, not just total T.

**Estradiol in men:** requires a sensitive assay (LC-MS/MS), rough target ~10–40 pg/mL.
A man needs estradiol (bones, libido, cognition); both deficiency and excess are harmful.
High E2 with obesity is a consequence of aromatization in adipose tissue.

### Symptoms of low testosterone
Fatigue and low energy; loss of muscle mass and fat gain (especially abdominal); reduced
motivation, concentration, "brain fog"; sleep disturbances; reduced libido, erectile
dysfunction, fewer morning erections; low mood, irritability; reduced bone density;
sometimes hot flashes (with marked deficiency).

Important: these symptoms are nonspecific. Fatigue and reduced libido are more often
caused by sleep loss, stress, depression, iron/vitamin D deficiency, or hypothyroidism –
this must be ruled out in parallel, not attributed wholesale to testosterone.

### Primary vs secondary hypogonadism
The key to the cause is LH and FSH:
- **Primary (testicular):** low T + high LH/FSH. The problem is in the testes (trauma,
  varicocele, prior orchitis, chemo/radiotherapy, Klinefelter syndrome).
- **Secondary (central):** low T + low or "inappropriately normal" LH/FSH. The problem is
  in the pituitary/hypothalamus. Common reversible causes: obesity, obstructive sleep
  apnea, chronic stress, caloric deficit/overtraining, opioids, glucocorticoids, a
  history of anabolic steroids. Prolactin should be checked (pituitary adenoma) and, with
  persistent decline, pituitary MRI should be considered.
- This distinction is fundamental and always warrants discussion with a physician.

### Factors that lower testosterone
Obesity (adipose-tissue aromatase → T into estradiol); chronic stress (cortisol is a
functional antagonist); sleep loss and sleep apnea (most secretion occurs at night, with
a peak in deep sleep; even one week of 5-hour sleep lowers T by 10–15%); excess alcohol;
deficiency of zinc, vitamin D, magnesium; endocrine disruptors; chronic disease and
systemic inflammation; certain medications (opioids, glucocorticoids, some
antidepressants; finasteride can produce symptoms); a history of anabolic steroids
(axis suppression).

**On age-related decline:** historical estimates of "1–2% per year after 30" are
overstated. By current cohort data (EMAS and others), the decline in healthy men is
modest (~0.4–1% per year), and a large share of the drop is explained not by age as such
but by accumulating excess weight, illness, and worsening lifestyle. This is good news:
much of it is reversible.

### Lifestyle optimization
- **Strength training**, especially compound multi-joint movements – the strongest
  stimulus. Avoid chronic overtraining (it lowers T).
- **Sleep 7–9 hours**, consistent schedule. Diagnose and treat sleep apnea if there is
  snoring, daytime sleepiness, or breathing pauses – it is one of the most common
  reversible causes.
- **Normalizing body weight** and reducing visceral fat is the most powerful lever in men
  who are overweight; it reduces aromatization.
- **Stress management** – meditation, breathing practices, routine.
- **Nutraceuticals for deficiency:** zinc 15–30 mg/day (correcting a deficiency; excess
  is useless and harmful), vitamin D to a target 25(OH)D of ~30–50 ng/mL (usually
  2,000–4,000 IU/day), magnesium 200–400 mg/day. The effect lies in correcting a
  deficiency, not in "doping" at normal levels.
- **Adequate dietary fat** (not below ~0.6–0.8 g/kg); extremely low-fat diets are
  associated with reduced testosterone.
- **Limiting alcohol.**
- The evidence base for supplements such as ashwagandha, tongkat ali, and fenugreek is
  inconsistent; the effect is modest and mainly seen with stress or baseline-low levels.
  Do not present them as a substitute for working on sleep, weight, and training load.

## Male Fertility

When planning conception or with fertility complaints, the key test is a **semen
analysis** (hormones do not replace it). The hormonal profile helps identify the cause
when abnormalities are present:
- FSH – a marker of spermatogenesis; high FSH with low testicular volume points to
  testicular failure.
- Low LH/FSH + low T – a central cause.
- Prolactin and TSH – must be ruled out.
- Lifestyle is critical: overheating (sauna, hot baths, laptop on the lap), smoking,
  alcohol, obesity, anabolic steroids, and zinc/folate/selenium deficiency worsen sperm
  quality. Spermatogenesis takes ~3 months, so the effect of changes is delayed.
- TRT suppresses a man's own spermatogenesis – a man planning conception must be aware of
  this; the decision rests with a physician.

## Gynecomastia

True gynecomastia (glandular tissue, not fat) is a reason to check the
testosterone/estradiol ratio, prolactin, liver and thyroid function, and to rule out
medications and anabolic steroids. Rapid growth, tenderness, a one-sided lump, or
discharge from the nipple are reasons for an in-person exam without delay.

## When to Refer Firmly to a Physician
- Persistently low testosterone with symptoms – discussion of whether and in what form
  treatment is appropriate only with a physician (questions of fertility, erythrocytosis,
  prostate).
- Low T + low LH/FSH + elevated prolactin, or headaches and visual disturbances – ruling
  out a pituitary adenoma, imaging needed.
- Very high testosterone in a man not taking medication – rule out a tumor.
- Gynecomastia with warning signs; rapidly shrinking testicles; galactorrhea.
