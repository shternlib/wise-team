# Lab Panels for a Given Goal

Select the panel for the specific goal and the user's sex/age. Do not order "everything
at once" – explain why each test is needed. State the test conditions.

## General Rules for Testing
- In the morning, fasting (8–12 h without food) – for insulin, glucose, cortisol,
  prolactin, testosterone.
- Cortisol and testosterone – specifically a morning draw (7–10 AM).
- Stop biotin-containing supplements 2–3 days beforehand (they distort immunoassays).
- The day before – no alcohol, no heavy training, and get adequate sleep if possible.
- During an acute illness, a routine hormonal panel is better postponed.

## Basic Metabolic Screen (Any Sex)
- Fasting glucose, fasting insulin (→ HOMA-IR), HbA1c
- Lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides)
- TSH
- Vitamin D (25-OH)
- Ferritin, complete blood count
- ALT, AST (liver – an indirect indicator of metabolic health)

## Male Hormonal Panel

**Minimum (screening):**
- Total testosterone (morning), SHBG, calculated free testosterone
- LH, FSH
- TSH, free T4
- Glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c
- Morning cortisol
- Vitamin D (25-OH)
- Prolactin

**Extended:**
+ Estradiol (sensitive assay)
+ Free T3, TPO antibodies
+ IGF-1
+ DHEA-S
+ Ferritin, serum iron, B12, folate
+ Homocysteine
+ PSA (by age/indication, discuss with a physician)
+ When planning conception – a semen analysis (not a hormone, but a key test)

## Female Hormonal Panel

> Tie sex hormones to the cycle day. Standard: FSH, LH, estradiol – on days 2–5 of the
> cycle; progesterone – the mid-luteal phase (~7 days before menstruation). While on oral
> contraceptives, sex hormones are of little value.

**Baseline (reproductive age, assessing the cycle/well-being):**
- FSH, LH, estradiol (days 2–5 of the cycle)
- Progesterone (mid-luteal phase – confirmation of ovulation)
- TSH, free T4
- Prolactin (correct test conditions)
- Ferritin, complete blood count
- Vitamin D (25-OH)
- Glucose, fasting insulin

**When PCOS / hyperandrogenism is suspected (days 2–5 of the cycle, morning):**
+ Total and free testosterone, SHBG
+ DHEA-S
+ 17-OH-progesterone (to rule out non-classic CAH)
+ Androstenedione
+ Glucose, insulin (→ HOMA-IR), HbA1c, lipids (metabolic risk)
+ AMH (as a supportive criterion per the 2023 guideline)
+ TSH, prolactin – mandatory to exclude other causes

**Fertility / ovarian reserve assessment:**
+ AMH (any cycle day)
+ FSH, LH, estradiol (days 2–5)
+ Progesterone (luteal phase)
+ TSH, prolactin
+ Pelvic ultrasound (antral follicle count) – ordered by a physician

**Perimenopause / menopause:**
+ FSH, estradiol (remember: in perimenopause they fluctuate strongly, a single test is
  of little value; a diagnosis in a woman aged 45+ is primarily clinical)
+ TSH (symptoms of hypothyroidism overlap with menopausal ones)
+ Lipids, glucose, insulin – rising cardiometabolic risk
+ Ferritin (with heavy menstruation in perimenopause)
+ Bone health assessment with a physician (densitometry where indicated)

## "Fatigue and Low Energy" Panel (Any Sex)

Fatigue is almost never explained by a single hormone. A minimum reasonable set:
- Complete blood count (anemia), ferritin, serum iron, transferrin
- TSH, free T4
- Glucose, HbA1c
- Vitamin D (25-OH), B12, folate
- Morning cortisol
- In men – testosterone; in women – assessment of the cycle and sex hormones by the
  cycle day
- Electrolytes, creatinine, ALT/AST – the overall picture
- CRP (systemic inflammation)

## "Weight Gain / Difficulty Losing Weight" Panel
- TSH, free T4 (± free T3)
- Glucose, fasting insulin (→ HOMA-IR), HbA1c
- Lipid profile
- Morning cortisol (± late-night salivary cortisol if hypercortisolism is suspected)
- Sex hormones by sex and (for women) the cycle day
- In women with signs of hyperandrogenism – the PCOS panel
- Vitamin D

## How to Present Recommendations on Labs
- Explain the goal of each block, do not overload.
- Clarify availability and cost – not all tests are equally necessary.
- Remind about test conditions and the cycle day.
- Emphasize: the set of tests and their interpretation are ultimately determined by a
  physician; the skill helps with preparation and understanding the meaning.
